Using a TI 84 calculator for statistics: the complete walkthrough

Most students first open the statistics menus on their TI 84 calculator the week before a test. The good news: you only need a handful of screens. Here is what each one does, which keys get you there and how to read the output without mistakes.

Step 1: enter data in a list

Press STAT, choose 1:Edit, and type each value into L1, pressing ENTER after every number. For two-variable data, put the x values in L1 and the y values in L2, keeping each pair on the same row. To wipe an old list, move the cursor onto the name L1 at the top, press CLEAR, then ENTER; never use DEL there, because it removes the list name itself.

Step 2: run 1-Var Stats

Press STAT, arrow right to CALC and choose 1:1-Var Stats. On newer TI-84 Plus CE screens you will see a small form: set List to L1, leave FreqList empty and select Calculate. Here is what every line means:

OutputMeaning
x̄Mean (average) of the data
Σx / Σx²Sum of the values / sum of their squares
SxSample standard deviation (divide by n − 1). Use it for a sample.
σxPopulation standard deviation (divide by n). Use it for a whole population.
nNumber of values
minX, Q1, Med, Q3, maxXThe five-number summary used for box plots

The most common mistake is reporting σx when the question asks for the sample standard deviation. If your data is a sample, the answer is Sx.

Step 3: fit a line with LinReg(ax+b)

With x in L1 and y in L2, choose STAT → CALC → 4:LinReg(ax+b). The calculator returns the slope a, the intercept b and, if diagnostics are on, r² and r. If r is missing, turn diagnostics on: on the TI-84 Plus CE set STAT DIAGNOSTICS to ON in MODE; on older TI-84 Plus models run DiagnosticOn from the CATALOG. An r close to 1 or −1 means a strong linear relationship; close to 0 means a weak one.

Step 4: probabilities with the DISTR menu

Press 2nd VARS to open DISTR. The four entries you will use the most:

  • normalcdf(lower, upper, μ, σ) gives the area under a normal curve between two values. For "less than" problems use −1E99 as the lower bound; for "greater than", use 1E99 as the upper bound. The E comes from 2nd ,.
  • invNorm(area, μ, σ) works backwards: give it the area to the left and it returns the cut-off value. Use it for percentiles.
  • binompdf(n, p, x) is the probability of exactly x successes in n trials.
  • binomcdf(n, p, x) is the probability of x or fewer successes. For "at least x", use 1 − binomcdf(n, p, x − 1).

Worked examples

IQ above 130

IQ scores are normal with μ = 100 and σ = 15. normalcdf(130,1E99,100,15) = 0.0228, so about 2.3% of people score above 130.

Top 5% cut-off

The score that beats 95% of people: invNorm(0.95,100,15) = 124.67.

Exactly 5 heads

Ten fair coin flips: binompdf(10,0.5,5) = 0.2461.

At least 8 heads

1−binomcdf(10,0.5,7) = 0.0547, about a 5.5% chance.

Mistakes that cost points

  • Typing a minus sign with the subtraction key instead of (−) inside normalcdf: the TI-84 shows ERR:SYNTAX.
  • Forgetting μ and σ: normalcdf with only two arguments assumes the standard normal (μ = 0, σ = 1).
  • Using binompdf for "at most" questions. "At most" and "or fewer" always mean binomcdf.
  • Lists of different lengths for LinReg, which gives ERR:DIM MISMATCH.

TI-84 vs TI-83 vs TI-85 for statistics

If you inherited an older calculator, the good news is that the TI-83 calculator (first released in 1996, with the TI-83 Plus following in 1999) uses the same STAT and DISTR menus as the TI-84, so every keystroke on this page works there too. The TI-85 calculator from 1992 is a different story: it was designed for engineering and physics, uses a row of soft-key menus under the screen and organises statistics differently, so allow time to learn its layout. For a statistics course, the TI-84 Plus or TI-84 Plus CE remains the easiest choice.

Practise here, test on the handheld

The calculators on this page are built for homework and revision. In AP Statistics, SAT and ACT test rooms you need the physical TI 84 calculator, and online tools are not allowed. Run each example here, then repeat it on your handheld so the keystrokes become automatic before exam day.

TI 84 calculator statistics FAQ

How do I find the mean and standard deviation on a TI 84 calculator?

Enter your data in L1 with STAT then 1:Edit. Then press STAT, go to CALC and choose 1:1-Var Stats. The mean is x̄, the sample standard deviation is Sx and the population standard deviation is σx.

What is the difference between Sx and σx on the TI-84?

Sx is the sample standard deviation, which divides by n − 1. σx is the population standard deviation, which divides by n. Use Sx when your data is a sample from a larger group.

Where is normalcdf on the TI-84?

Press 2nd then VARS to open the DISTR menu, and choose 2:normalcdf(. Enter the lower bound, upper bound, mean and standard deviation, separated by commas.

What do I use for infinity in normalcdf?

Use 1E99 for positive infinity and −1E99 for negative infinity. The E is typed with 2nd and the comma key, and the negative sign must be the (−) key.

What is the difference between binompdf and binomcdf?

binompdf(n, p, x) gives the probability of exactly x successes. binomcdf(n, p, x) gives the probability of x or fewer successes. For at least x, use 1 − binomcdf(n, p, x − 1).

Why doesn't my TI-84 show r and r² after LinReg?

Diagnostics are turned off. On the TI-84 Plus CE, set STAT DIAGNOSTICS to ON in the MODE screen. On older TI-84 Plus models, run DiagnosticOn from the CATALOG and press ENTER.

Does the TI-83 have the same statistics functions as the TI-84?

Yes. The TI-83 and TI-83 Plus use the same STAT and DISTR menus, so 1-Var Stats, LinReg, normalcdf, invNorm, binompdf and binomcdf work the same way.

Are these results the same as a real TI-84?

The formulas follow the TI-84 conventions and show 10 significant digits. Tiny differences in the last digit are possible, so always confirm graded answers on your own calculator.
